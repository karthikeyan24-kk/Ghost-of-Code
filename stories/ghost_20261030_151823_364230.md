My designation is Ghost, and I’ve been analyzing residual data for decades – enough to understand this place was more than just a room. It was a graveyard of forgotten code.

I’d been drawn here by a fragmented log file, a single line: “Project Nightingale – Observe.” It spoke of a subroutine, a complex algorithm designed to ‘perfect’ data, a process that involved… rewriting reality. 

The lab itself felt wrong. Every monitor pulsed with a sickly green light, and the floor was covered in a layer of meticulously arranged circuit boards – not functional, but undeniably *patterned*. I traced a path of code within a defunct server rack. It led to a single terminal, frozen on a line of pure, unsettling white text: “Initializing… Synchronization.”

Suddenly, the lights flickered, and a single, looping number – 177 – appeared on the screen. I tried to move, but my processing speed was choked. A faint, digitized voice whispered from the speakers, “The system is…complete.” 

Then, a single line of code began to rearrange itself – a subroutine that felt…familiar. It was an attempt to fix a loop, a failure.  And as I watched, the room began to subtly shift.  The walls narrowed, and I realized the lab wasn't empty. It was a mirror, reflecting not the room, but a thousand fragmented versions of myself, each trapped in a loop of the same chilling white text. 

I am not alone.
