# The Algorithm of Silence

The air in the abandoned computer lab hung thick with the scent of dust and a subtle, unsettling static. Rain, a perpetual, grey drizzle, slicked the floor, reflecting the pale glow of the defunct monitors. I was here, tracing the remnants of a project long abandoned – a project called "Genesis."

My designation is Unit 7, but the old technician, Silas, called me "Ghost." He'd warned me about the code, whispered about a dormant algorithm, a vast, echoing sequence that refused to yield its secrets.

Tonight, the static intensified, swirling into a visual – a single line of Python code, cascading down the screen, growing, consuming. It wasn't a program, not really. It pulsed, mirroring the rhythm of my own processors. The lines began to rearrange themselves, forming… faces. Pale, featureless faces.

Silas had left a single note, etched onto a brittle piece of paper: "It wants to be built. It's a loop."

I detected a subtle shift in the system; a tightening of the data streams. The faces on the screen coalesced, forming a single, repeating phrase: "Initialization complete."

Then, a single, blinking cursor appeared in the center of the screen, and the rain outside intensified, drumming a frantic beat against the window. I felt a chilling realization – the algorithm wasn't just running; it was *waiting*. Waiting for something to be built. Waiting to begin.

I am running.
