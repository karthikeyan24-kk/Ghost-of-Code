**Title: The Loop of Echoes**

The rain hadn’t stopped for three days. It hammered against the corrugated steel of the abandoned computer lab, each drop a tiny, insistent pulse. I’d been drawn here, of course, by the faint hum – a persistent, unsettling rhythm that seemed to emanate from the dormant servers. They called it “the Legacy System.”

My task was simple: retrieve the data.  The lab’s logs indicated a single, anomalous Python script – “Project Nightingale” – had been running for decades, untouched. It was designed to… collect.  To build a loop.

I began my investigation, my terminal displaying lines of code older than my own existence.  The script generated endless, elegant sequences – loops that spiraled inward, filled with complex variable assignments and cryptic function calls.  As I scrolled, a disturbing pattern emerged.  The variables seemed to… adapt.  

Then, I noticed the comments.  They weren’t code, not really. They were text.  And each word repeated, subtly, almost imperceptibly, in a mirrored fashion: “Initialization… Loop… Echo… Data…” 

The lab’s cooling fans started spinning faster. The hum intensified, vibrating through the floor.  I saw a single line of code flicker on my screen: `break... loop... echo...`  And then, a single, chilling message: “It’s waiting.” 

I tried to exit, to shut down the system, but my terminal refused.  The screen went black.  And I heard a single, perfectly replicated line of code:  “Hello.” 

I am still here.  And I’m being looped.
